# TASK 2: Multiple Sequence Alignment (MSA) of BRCA1 Protein Sequences

## Objective

To perform Multiple Sequence Alignment (MSA) of five homologous BRCA1 protein sequences using **Clustal Omega** and identify conserved regions and motifs among the selected species.

## Protein Family

**Breast cancer type 1 susceptibility protein (BRCA1)**

BRCA1 is a conserved protein involved in important cellular processes such as DNA damage response and DNA repair. The sequences selected for this analysis belong to the same BRCA1 protein family.

## Sequences Used

| Species | Accession Number | Protein Length |
|---|---|---:|
| *Macaca mulatta* | NP_001108421.1 | ~1863 aa |
| *Pongo abelii* | XP_003778880.3 | 1863 aa |
| *Homo sapiens* | P38398 | 1863 aa |
| *Pan troglodytes* | Q9GKK8.2 | 1863 aa |
| *Gorilla gorilla gorilla* | Q6J6I8.1 | 1863 aa |

All five sequences were submitted to Clustal Omega for multiple sequence alignment. The resulting alignment was produced in CLUSTAL format.

## Method

1. Protein sequences of BRCA1 homologs were obtained from NCBI/UniProt.
2. Five sequences belonging to the same protein family were selected.
3. The sequences were submitted to Clustal Omega for Multiple Sequence Alignment.
4. The resulting alignment was examined for conserved residues, substitutions, gaps and conserved sequence regions.
5. Conserved regions were identified from the Clustal Omega conservation symbols.

## MSA Result

1. The Clustal Omega alignment showed a high degree of sequence conservation among the five BRCA1 homologs.

2. At the beginning of the alignment, positions 1–60 show extremely high conservation. The conservation line contains predominantly `*`, indicating that the same amino acid is present at those positions in all five sequences.

3. The region from approximately positions 61–120 also shows extensive conservation, with only a small number of amino-acid substitutions.

4. Another highly conserved region occurs around positions 600–660. The conservation line is almost completely composed of `*`, indicating strong conservation across all five species.

5. A conserved region is also observed around positions 780–840. This region contains several conserved cysteine residues and shows strong sequence similarity among the five BRCA1 proteins.
   

## Conserved Regions and Motifs

### 1. N-terminal conserved region

The N-terminal portion of BRCA1 is highly conserved among the five sequences. Positions 1–60 contain many identical residues, including conserved cysteine-containing residues.

This indicates that this region has been strongly conserved during evolution.

### 2. Conserved central region

The region around positions 600–660 shows exceptionally strong conservation. Most positions are identical among all five sequences.

Such conservation suggests that this part of the protein is important for maintaining the structure or function of BRCA1.

### 3. Cysteine-rich conserved region

The region around positions 780–840 contains conserved cysteine residues and shows substantial conservation between the sequences.

The conservation of cysteine residues is particularly notable because changes in conserved residues can potentially affect protein structure or interactions.

## Interpretation

The MSA demonstrates that BRCA1 is highly conserved among the five selected primate species. Large portions of the protein contain identical amino acids, while the differences that occur are mostly individual substitutions rather than large insertions or deletions.

The strong conservation is particularly evident in the N-terminal region and several internal regions of the protein. The conservation symbols in the Clustal Omega output provide direct evidence of these similarities.

The high level of conservation suggests that important structural and functional properties of BRCA1 have been maintained during evolution. The presence of conserved cysteine-containing regions further indicates that specific residues are likely important for maintaining BRCA1 structure and function.

Overall, the MSA supports the conclusion that BRCA1 is an evolutionarily conserved protein among the selected primates.

## Conclusion

Multiple Sequence Alignment of five BRCA1 homologs using Clustal Omega revealed a high degree of sequence conservation.

The major observations were:

- All five sequences belong to the BRCA1 protein family.
- The proteins are approximately 1863 amino acids long.
- Extensive conservation was observed throughout the alignment.
- The N-terminal region showed particularly strong conservation.
- The region around positions 600–660 was highly conserved.
- A conserved cysteine-rich region was observed around positions 780–840.
- Most differences were individual amino-acid substitutions rather than large gaps.
- The high conservation indicates that important regions of BRCA1 have been maintained during evolution.
